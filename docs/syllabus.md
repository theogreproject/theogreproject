# SYLLABUS — The Ogre Project (v0.2)

## 1) Course identification

**Course title:** _The Ogre Project — Tabletop RPG Formation: From Player to Published GM_
**Format:** Self-paced, project-based learning, with deliberate practice (drills)
**Target audience:** Beginners and intermediates (players who want to GM; GMs who want a method and depth)
**Systems:** System-agnostic
**Primary outcome:** A portfolio of playable and publishable RPG materials

---

## 2) Course purpose

To train people to **play well** and **GM well with consistency**, developing skills in:

- table facilitation (social contract, safety, rhythm),
- session design and structured improvisation,
- encounter and challenge design (combat and non-combat),
- campaign building (from one-shots to long-running play, across styles),
- functional worldbuilding (built for the table),
- and converting materials into **publishable products**.

---

## 3) Learning objectives

By the end of the course, learners will be able to:

1. Act as a player who **improves the table** (collaboration, spotlight-sharing, improv, clear intent).
2. Run Session Zero and maintain a healthy table (safety, conflict handling, pacing/energy, debrief).
3. Prepare and run high-quality sessions with **low-prep** methods (scenes, clues, choices).
4. Design varied, fair challenges (including puzzles/traps/hazards, mobs, and monster control).
5. Build campaigns in multiple styles (dungeon crawler, hexcrawl, West Marches, intrigue, war, mystery, crossing the streams, etc.), integrating **PC arcs**.
6. Create worlds “from the ground up” with coherence and usability, including macro-geography.
7. Organize, playtest, and package content for publication (PDF/web/zine, etc.).

---

## 4) Core competencies

- **Collaborative play and RPG improv** (offers, reincorporation, status, conflict-in-scene).
- **Fun literacy**: identifying play preferences and designing for mixed tables.
- **Session design**: scenes, tension, stakes, information flow, consequences (“fail-forward”).
- **Challenge design**: clarity, fair telegraphing, perceived vs actual difficulty, rewards and progression (XP/milestone).
- **Campaign operations**: factions, clocks, off-screen world motion, recurring NPCs, purposeful random encounters.
- **Monster/NPC control**: personality, goals, triggers, and simple combat scripts.
- **Functional cartography**: tactical space (battlemaps), exploration space (regional maps), macro coherence (world maps).
- **Writing and product craft**: table usability, editorial consistency, playtesting, and publication.

---

## 5) Course structure (tracks and embedded map modules)

**Official recommended sequence:** 0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6

### Track 0 — Player

- DMG-inspired player types (actor, explorer, fighter, instigator, optimizer, problem solver, storyteller, etc.)
- Improv for RPG, spotlight skills, rules etiquette, “playable character” construction (flags), genre preferences.

### Track 1 — Table

- Session Zero, table contract, and expectation alignment (including genre/style).
- Safety and conduct, focus/energy management, conflict handling, debrief practices.

### Track 2 — Session

- Minimal prep, structured improvisation, scene design, cluecraft and choice-driven play (node-based structures).
- Recurring “clinics” for real table issues (e.g., **doors/decision paralysis**, **PC death**, **dungeon pacing**, **random encounters**).

### Track 3 — Challenges & Conflict

- Purposeful combat and non-combat challenges; balance; XP vs milestone; hazards/exhaustion/environment; puzzles/traps; mobs; set pieces.
- **Monster/NPC control in combat** (personality, goals, triggers, scripts).
- **Map Module A: Battlemaps & Tactical Space**

### Track 4 — Campaigns

- One-shot → mini → long; campaign engines (factions/clocks); recurring NPCs; encounters and purposeful random encounters; PC arcs; season management.
- Campaign styles: dungeon crawler, hexcrawl, West Marches, intrigue, horror, mystery, war, swashbuckling, crossing the streams, etc.
- **Map Module B: Regional Maps & Travel/Exploration**

### Track 5 — Worldbuilding

- “From the ground up” methods: world truths, layered scope (table→city→region→world), usable history, geography→culture/politics/economy, cosmology when needed.
- **Map Module C: World Maps & Macro Coherence**

### Track 6 — Publication

- Writing for the table, editorial structure, playtesting/iteration, layout/packaging, assets/attribution, formats and distribution.

---

## 6) Instructional approach

- **Deliberate practice:** short drills focused on a single skill.
- **Cumulative projects:** each track produces portfolio artifacts.
- **Case studies and anti-patterns:** why things work, why they fail at the table.
- **Improvement loop:** run → debrief → adjust → run better.
- **Curated “best-of” techniques:** established approaches from the hobby, compared and converted into procedures.

---

## 7) Assessment (portfolio-based)

Completion is demonstrated through deliverables and a minimum “definition of done” standard.

**Primary capstones:**

1. **Player Playbook** (profile + flags + improv + collaboration)
2. **Session Zero & Table Kit** (contract + safety + scripts + debrief)
3. **Complete One-Shot** (session + challenges + battlemaps where appropriate)
4. **Mini-Campaign (3–6 sessions)** (chosen style + PC arcs + regional map + campaign engines)
5. **Setting Brief** (functional worldbuilding + optional world map)
6. **Publishable Package** (adventure/campaign/setting in a consistent editorial format)

---

## COURSE DESCRIPTION (EMENTA) — (v0.2)

A practical, project-based formation in tabletop RPG play and game mastering, from player fundamentals to publishable content. Covers collaborative play and RPG improv, fun literacy (DMG-style player types), table facilitation and safety, minimal-prep session design (scenes, clues, choice structures), and challenge design (combat and non-combat), including balance and progression (XP/milestone), hazards/exhaustion, puzzles and traps, mobs, and monster control. Develops campaign-building skills across multiple styles (dungeon crawler, hexcrawl, West Marches, intrigue, horror, mystery, war, swashbuckling, and **crossing the streams**), with emphasis on campaign engines (factions/clocks), purposeful random encounters, recurring NPCs, and PC arcs. Integrates functional cartography through embedded map modules (battlemaps, regional maps, world maps) and “from the ground up” worldbuilding aimed at table usability. Concludes with writing for the table, playtesting, revision, packaging, and pathways to publication.

## Official Track Sequence (proposal v1)

### 0. **Player**

**Why first:** this is the human + technical foundation. Before GMing, a person needs to know how to:

- collaborate, share spotlight, and communicate intent,
- improvise (accept offers, reincorporate, build scenes),
- understand preferences (DMG-style player types) and genres.

> **Result:** the learner immediately improves any table — even without GMing yet.

---

### 1. **Table**

**Why after Player:** once the learner understands the “social game,” they learn to **facilitate**:

- Session Zero / table contract,
- safety and conflict handling,
- pacing and spotlight as the facilitator’s responsibility.

> **Result:** they can already run a healthy table before mastering “content.”

---

### 2. **Session**

**Why now:** with a solid social foundation, we move into the practical craft:

- minimal prep,
- structured improvisation,
- scene design,
- cluecraft and choice-driven play (no rails).

> **Result:** they can run strong sessions with low prep (the GM core loop).

---

### 3. **Challenges & Conflict**

**Why before Campaigns:** here the learner builds high-quality “playable content”:

- purposeful combat and non-combat,
- balance/difficulty (including XP vs milestone),
- hazards / exhaustion / environment,
- puzzles / traps,
- mobs,
- monster/NPC control during combat.
  ✅ **Map Module A (Battlemaps & Tactical Space)** fits here.

> **Result:** sessions gain consistency and variety; encounters stop feeling “kind of random.”

---

### 4. **Campaigns**

**Why now:** once session-running and challenges are solid, we can scale up:

- one-shot → mini → long-form,
- engines (factions/clocks),
- styles (dungeon crawler, hexcrawl, West Marches, intrigue, war, mystery…),
- **crossing the streams** as a campaign style,
- well-used random encounters,
- recurring NPCs,
- **PC arcs** as a campaign engine.
  ✅ **Map Module B (Regional Maps & Travel/Exploration)** fits here.

> **Result:** the learner stops “planning a plot” and starts “operating a moving world.”

---

### 5. **Worldbuilding**

**Why after Campaigns:** worldbuilding becomes much better once you already know **what a campaign needs**:

- world truths,
- layers of scope (table → city → region → world),
- usable history,
- geography → culture,
- cosmology when needed.
  ✅ **Map Module C (World Maps & Macro Coherence)** fits here.

> **Result:** the world becomes infrastructure, not an encyclopedia.

---

### 6. **Publication**

**Why last:** now the learner has material worth packaging:

- writing for the table,
- editorial structure,
- playtest/iteration,
- layout/packaging,
- assets/attribution,
- formats and distribution.

> **Result:** a real portfolio + a pipeline for publishing.

---

## Quick notes (to ensure it covers your “recurring table topics”)

- **Doors / decision paralysis:** appears in Track 2 (Session) and returns in Track 3 (dungeons/hazards).
- **PC death:** Track 1 (contract/expectations) + Track 2/3 (procedures and consequences).
- **Dungeons / exploration / environmental effects / exhaustion / hazards:** Track 3 (and ties to Map Module A, then later to regional mapping).
- **Mobs and recurring NPCs:** Track 3 (mobs/control) + Track 4 (recurrence/agenda).
- **Monster control:** a strong sub-block inside Track 3.
- **Genres:** a lens starting in Track 0 and becoming an explicit tool in Track 4.
