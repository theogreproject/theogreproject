Welcome, traveler! **The Ogre Project** is a **learn-by-doing curriculum** for **TTRPG players and Game Masters**.

This is not a wiki and not a lore museum. It’s a course:
you’ll learn by **building small, usable artifacts**, one quest at a time.

No gatekeeping. No “read 900 pages first”. You can start where you are and still ship meaningful work.

## Who this is for

- **New to TTRPGs:** learn how to play well and support the table.
- **New GMs:** run your first sessions with confidence.
- **Experienced GMs:** tighten your method, prep smarter, and design better adventures.

## Before you begin

You only need a few things:

- A place to write (notebook, Notion, Google Doc, a piece of paper... anything you’ll actually use).
- A “default lens” system for examples (D&D, PF2e, Tormenta20, OSR… pick one).
- A timer.
- Permission to ship imperfect drafts.

### Quick checklist

- [ ] I picked **one** system to use as my default lens (for now).
- [ ] I have a place to store my artifacts (a folder, a doc, a repo).
- [ ] I’m okay shipping something “good enough”.

## Your study routine

Here’s the loop. Don’t overthink it:

<Steps>
  1. **Read** a lesson (look for the decision it teaches). 2. **Practice** the exercise (constraints
  are training wheels). 3. **Ship** the deliverable (save/publish it somewhere you can reuse later).
</Steps>

## First deliverable (10–20 minutes)

Your first quest is a tiny artifact called a **Campaign Promise**.

### Deliverable format

Write:

1. **One paragraph** describing what your table is _about_ (the promise).
2. **Three bullets**:

- **Players will…** (what they do most sessions)
- **The vibe is…** (tone/genre in plain words)
- **We avoid…** (one or two things you don’t want at the table)

### Template (copy/paste)

**Campaign Promise (1 paragraph):**

> …

**3 bullets:**

- Players will…
- The vibe is…
- We avoid…

<Aside type="note">
  Save this somewhere easy to find. You’ll reference it when writing hooks, factions, and sessions.
</Aside>

### Optional reflection (2 minutes)

Answer these (quick and messy is fine):

- What kind of experience do you want to create at your table?
- What do you struggle with most right now (prep, improv, pacing, rules, worldbuilding…)?
