## Introduction

Most people don’t fail at TTRPG learning because they’re “not creative”.
They fail because learning turns into **hoarding**: tabs, notes, videos, ideas… and nothing ever becomes play.

The Quest Loop is the cure. It’s simple on purpose:
a rhythm you can repeat every week without burning out.

## Lesson

### Read (input with intention)

Reading is useful when you’re hunting for **one decision** or **one method**.
If you read to “understand everything”, you’ll never feel done.

A good reading session ends with one sentence:

> “The thing I’m practicing this week is: ____.”

### Practice (constraints beat motivation)

Practice works best when you remove options.
Constraints are not a limitation — they’re training wheels.

If your practice feels vague, it’s usually because the task is too open.
Shrink it until it becomes impossible to fail.

### Ship (small, usable artifacts)

Shipping means saving something you can **reuse at the table**.

Not “notes about a cool idea”.
Not “a concept of a plan”.
A small artifact: a hook list, a faction card, a scene outline, a checklist.

<Aside type="note">
If it’s bigger than one screen, it might be a project — not a weekly ship.
</Aside>

## Assignment

### Required

1. Re-read **Start Here** and confirm you have a place to store artifacts.
2. Read **How this curriculum works** and commit to one weekly rhythm.
3. Do the exercise: **Learning Log (Quest Loop)**.

### Optional

- Skim any one article/video you like about “learning by shipping” and write 2 bullets on what you’ll copy.

## Knowledge check

Answer in 1–2 sentences:

- Why do constraints help you practice?
- What’s the difference between “a cool idea” and “a shipped artifact”?
- If you only have one session this week, what do you do?
